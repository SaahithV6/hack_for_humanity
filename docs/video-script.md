## I — The problem · 0:59

> It's twenty past midnight. You've been on your phone for two hours. You
> stopped enjoying it around eleven, and you still can't put it down.

*Dark room. A thumb scrolling. Cut to a lock screen reading 12:21.*

> This isn't willpower. It's a loop. Stress makes the feed attractive. The feed
> hands you unpredictable little rewards — the same schedule a slot machine
> uses. And every hour in there, the thing you were avoiding gets worse. So the
> stress goes up, and the feed gets more attractive.

*Draw the loop as you describe it — three words and two arrows is enough.*

## II — The idea · 0:28

> This is Moth. You're the moth, the screen is the light. It helps you land.

*App launches. The buddy settles onto the screen.*

> It gives you the dopamine somewhere else, in small finishable actions, then at
> bedtime tells you what you did — so the day is accounted for.

*Quick flash: a task card, then the bedtime summary. Two seconds each.*

> It's built on behavioural activation, an established treatment for depression.
> You don't wait until you feel like it. You do the thing, and the motivation
> arrives after.

*Text on screen: **behavioural activation**. Judges score research grounding —
don't rush this line.*

## III — Every feature · 1:18

> Setup is six questions and a bedtime.

*Three fast cuts through onboarding. Don't linger.*

> Then Moth gives you one thing. Never a list — a list is something you scan,
> feel bad about, and close. One task, naming a physical action, saying where it
> ends.

*The home screen, one card. Let it be readable.*

> Done, or not this one. Skipping isn't failing, it's data. Moth learns which
> kinds of things you actually finish, at what time, at what energy, and stops
> offering the rest.

*Tap Done. Tap Not this one. Then open **What Moth learned** and let the bars
show.*

> **[CUT for 2:30]** It drops difficulty fast when you miss, because asking too
> much of someone already flat is how these apps lose people.

*Nothing specific — stay on the learned screen.*

> Already scrolling? One button, something you can start in ten seconds.
> Instantly — a spinner at midnight means you go back to the feed.

*Tap **I'm stuck scrolling**. The point is that nothing loads.*

> After five tasks you write your own. That's the real goal. Moth predicts from
> your own history, so it's taps, not typing.

*The countdown bar filling, then compose — tap two suggestions, no keyboard.*

> Then at your bedtime, it interrupts you. Tasks stop.

*Set bedtime a minute ahead beforehand so this fires on camera by itself.*

> And it reads your day back. Your actual tasks, in your words. If you did
> nothing, it says so kindly, because "you completed zero of four" is the exact
> sentence that starts the next scroll. Then goodnight. Nothing else in this app
> to look at.

*Let the summary reveal at its own pace. This is the emotional beat — don't talk
over the reveal.*
