# Moth — demo talking points

Not a script. Screen-record the app, talk over it, hit these in order. Riff —
the points matter, the wording doesn't. Aim for about two minutes.

## 1. The problem (~20s)

- It's midnight, you meant to sleep an hour ago, and you're not even enjoying it.
- It's not willpower. Stress makes the feed attractive, the feed makes the
  stress worse.
- It's worst at bedtime, because going to bed means sitting with a day you
  can't account for.

## 2. Click through it (~60s)

- Six questions and a bedtime. That's the whole setup.
- **One task at a time, never a list.** A list is something you scan and pick
  nothing from — same paralysis as the feed. Worth saying out loud; it's the
  main design call.
- Tap Done a couple of times.
- Hit **I'm stuck scrolling** — point out it's instant, no loading.
- Show **What Moth learned**: it works out what you actually do and drops the rest.
- After five tasks you write your own — and it autocompletes, so barely any typing.

## 3. Bedtime (~20s)

- At your bedtime it interrupts you.
- Reads the day back using what you actually did.
- Then goodnight — and there's nothing else in the app to look at.

## 4. The tech, fast (~20s)

- All on the phone. Few megabytes, works offline, runs on a 4GB phone.
- One optional cloud model writes only the goodnight.
- Everything it writes gets checked — invents a number or something you didn't
  do, it's thrown away.

---

**Turn on airplane mode while you're demoing.** Four seconds, no setup, and
it's the whole privacy argument — almost nobody else at this hackathon can show
their app working with the network off. Don't cut away while you do it.

**Say these once each, then stop:** "behavioural activation — the action comes
first, the motivation follows" · "no dependencies, 59 tests" · "built in 30
hours". Judges score the research grounding and it takes one sentence.

**Two things before you record.** Delete the app from the simulator first or
you'll launch past onboarding. And if the cloud toggle is on, hit the Render URL
once to wake it — the free tier sleeps and the app will quietly fall back to its
local summary.
